Differences in “Wet” Adsorption of CO2 for UiO-66-NH2-D1 and UiO-66-NH2-D2
To better understand the cause of the differences in wet adsorption of CO2 between UiO-66-
NH2-D1 and UiO-66-NH2-D2 a detailed analysis of the host-adsorbate and adsorbate-adsorbate 
energies was performed on each structure at identical water loadings (2 mol/kg) and identical 
pressures (pCO2 = 5 kPa). As shown by Figure S1B and S1C of the ESI, both defect structures show 
comparable CO2 adsorption when dry (~0.52 mol/kg at 5 kPa), however at 2 mol/kg water loading, 
defect 1 has a significantly higher adsorption of CO2 (~0.65 mol/kg at 5 kPa) compared with defect 
2 (~0.52 mol/kg at 5 kPa). Here, the number of adsorbed CO2 molecules in each MOF varies 
throughout the simulation, as shown by the CO2 adsorption histogram in Figure S8 below.  As 
expected, UiO-66-NH2-D1 has a higher mean adsorption compared to UiO-66-NH2-D2. 
Moreover, the overall energy of each system varies according to the number of adsorbent 
molecules in the system at a given instant. 
A code was written to determine the average energy of each system as a function of the number 
of adsorbed CO2 molecules. The difference in this energy (ED2 – ED1) is displayed in Figure S9 
below.  As expected, the total energy of UiO-66-NH2-D2 is higher than UiO-66-NH2-D1, meaning 
that CO2 adsorption is less favored for the former. Interestingly, this energy difference is a 
consistent 25 kT higher for D2, regardless of the number of CO2 molecules that are adsorbed. This 
energy includes the host-adsorbate and adsorbate-adsorbate contributions but not the host-host 
interactions. Importantly, this breakdown of the energies shows that the reason for D2’s higher 
energy relative to D1 is due to the large unfavourability of the host-adsorbate interactions in that 
system that outweigh the favorable adsorbate-adsorbate interaction differences.
